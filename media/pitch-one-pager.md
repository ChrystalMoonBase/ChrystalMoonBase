# Chrystal Moon Base — Partnership Proposal

An open research concept exploring autonomous lunar construction using in-situ resources, robotic systems, and distributed laser energy.

The goal is not to propose a finished system. It is to define a structured research programme that can be independently validated — and to do so openly, without exclusivity, without IP barriers, and without overstating what is currently known.

**ESA OSIP:** Qualified — Open for Discussion · 2026

---

## Why This Question Matters

Permanent lunar settlement cannot scale if it relies entirely on Earth-launched infrastructure. The economics become unworkable at permanent-habitation scale. In-situ resource utilisation is not one option among many — it is likely the only approach that could eventually sustain a permanent human presence.

Whether the specific architecture proposed by Chrystal Moon Base — autonomous robotic construction using laser sintering and polar solar energy — is viable is genuinely unknown. The purpose of the Phase 0 programme is to find out, honestly, with published results either way.

NASA, ESA, JAXA, and commercial operators are all developing ISRU capabilities. The engineering questions CMB is asking are questions the field needs answered regardless of which organisation eventually attempts permanent habitation.

## Phase 0 — Earth Validation Programme

Five independent demonstrators, each testable in existing laboratory or field environments, each independently publishable:

| Demonstrator | What It Tests | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| D1 — Laser Sintering | Whether regolith simulant can be sintered to structural quality at scale | The core construction process — if this fails, the concept fails |
| D2 — Power Beaming | Whether laser energy can be reliably delivered to a moving robot | The energy distribution method — undemonstrated at this scale |
| D3 — Robot Locomotion | Whether the CMB-R1 design can operate in simulated lunar conditions | No prototype exists yet |
| D4 — Swarm Coordination | Whether 4 robots can autonomously coordinate construction | No coordination software exists yet |
| D5 — Full Integration | Whether all systems work together over 72 hours | The pre-flight proof of concept |

An institution can engage with one demonstrator without committing to the others. A negative result from D1 is as valuable as a positive one — it would redirect the entire field away from a dead end.

## Collaboration Model

We are looking for distributed contributions, not a single large funder.

**Universities and research institutions:**
Materials testing, robotics algorithm development, structural modelling, sintering characterisation, swarm AI simulation. Real open questions, real publication credit, real contribution to a field that does not yet have answers.

**Space agencies:**
Technical review of open documents, data sharing where possible, ISRU programme alignment, formal engagement with Phase 0 results. No funding required to begin a conversation.

**Industry:**
Component testing in representative conditions, subsystem validation, technical advisory with full publication credit. In-kind contributions valued and documented.

**Research funders:**
Demonstrator 1 alone is estimated at €150,000–300,000. Full Phase 0 programme: €1.5–3 million over 3–5 years. All results published openly. Every euro publicly documented.

---

## What We Offer

- Full open publication of all results — positive and negative — with co-authorship credit for contributing institutions
- No IP restrictions — nothing developed here is patented; partners use all findings freely in their own work
- Named, specific contribution credit — documented by what was done, not just that a partner was involved
- Honest reporting — CMB does not overstate its progress or certainty; partners engage with a project that says clearly what it does not know and what might not work

---

## Three Constraints — Non-Negotiable

- No exclusivity agreements — with any partner, ever
- No patenting of results — all outputs remain open
- Full transparency — all experimental outcomes published, including failures
