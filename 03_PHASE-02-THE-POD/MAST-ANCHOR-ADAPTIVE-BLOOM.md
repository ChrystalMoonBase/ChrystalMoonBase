# Mast Anchor — Adaptive Bloom Variant
## Chrystal Moon Base — Phase 0.2 Structural Concept

**Status:** Concept proposal  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Concept

The Adaptive Bloom adds active adjustment to the Pure Bloom concept. Each leaf has a variable-angle hinge at its attachment point, allowing individual leaf angles to be adjusted by robot command before sintering. This allows the pod to create a level working platform on uneven terrain, and to optimise the leaf angle for maximum bearing surface area before the bond is made permanent.

---

## Relationship to v2.5 Configuration

The Adaptive Bloom anchor concept and the v2.5 levelling configuration described in POD-BLOOM-DEPLOYMENT.md are the same system. The variable hinge serves both the levelling function and the optimised anchoring function.

---

## Additional Capability

Beyond levelling, the adaptive hinge allows leaves to be angled downward into a slope rather than resting on it, potentially increasing the anchoring effectiveness on sloped terrain by directing load into the hillside rather than along it.

---

## Disadvantages

- **Mechanical complexity** — variable hinges are harder to design, test, and qualify than fixed hinges, especially for the temperature range and dust environment of the lunar surface
- **Additional mass** — actuators and lock mechanisms add mass to each leaf hinge
- **Failure mode** — a jammed variable hinge may prevent a leaf from deploying at all, reducing the foundation area

---

## Assessment

The Adaptive Bloom adds meaningful capability on uneven terrain at meaningful cost in complexity. The v2.5 variable hinge is included in the current preferred design (Rooted Hybrid) for levelling purposes. Whether to also use it for anchoring optimisation is an engineering trade that requires terrain data from the actual landing site.
