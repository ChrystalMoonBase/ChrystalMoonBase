# Mast Anchor — Rooted Hybrid (Preferred Concept)
## Chrystal Moon Base — Phase 0.2 Structural Concept

**Status:** Concept proposal — preferred by founder, final selection for engineers  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Overview

The Rooted Hybrid is the fourth and most complex of the four mast anchoring concepts. It combines two distinct anchoring mechanisms: a surface sintered foundation (the "bloom" of the pod leaves) and a subsurface anchoring system driven into the regolith below the pod.

The founder considers this the most likely candidate for the first flight, but emphasises that this is an intuition based on the logic of combining surface and subsurface anchoring — not an engineering determination. The final anchor selection must be made by engineers based on ground testing of all four variants.

---

## Design Concept

**Surface component — the pod bloom:**
The 8 pod leaves, sintered permanently to the lunar regolith by the robots as their first task, provide a wide surface foundation. This distributes the mast's lateral load (from solar curtain aerodynamic-equivalent force in dust storms, robot activities, and seismic micro-events) across a large footprint.

**Subsurface component — driven anchors:**
Below the pod central column, a set of anchoring rods or helical screws is driven into the regolith to a depth of 1–3 metres. These provide resistance to vertical uplift and supplement the lateral resistance of the surface foundation.

The two systems are mechanically connected through the pod central column, acting together as a single structural unit.

---

## Why "Hybrid"

Neither surface sintering alone nor subsurface anchoring alone is ideal:

- **Surface sintering alone** provides excellent lateral resistance but limited uplift resistance if regolith properties are weaker than expected.
- **Subsurface anchors alone** depend critically on regolith bearing capacity at depth, which is poorly characterised for the Peary rim specifically.
- **Combined:** each system compensates for the other's weakness. If surface sintering quality is lower than expected, the subsurface anchors provide backup. If subsurface conditions are poor, the sintered surface foundation carries more load.

---

## Why "Rooted"

The subsurface anchors are the "roots" — like a tree that is stable both because of its wide surface canopy (the sintered bloom) and its deep root system.

---

## Key Engineering Questions

1. What regolith compaction and bearing capacity should the subsurface anchors be designed for? Published data for highland regolith at Peary latitude is limited.
2. How are the anchors driven? Options include: mechanical screw (requires rotation drive), impact hammer (percussion), or thermal lance (heating creates a hole). Each has mass and reliability implications.
3. What depth is required for adequate anchoring? This is a function of regolith properties and the loads to be resisted.
4. How does the anchor system interact with the pod's landing leg system? The anchors must not interfere with landing leg deployment and must be deployable after landing is complete.
