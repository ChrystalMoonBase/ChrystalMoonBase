# Mast Anchor — Pure Bloom Variant
## Chrystal Moon Base — Phase 0.2 Structural Concept

**Status:** Concept proposal  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Concept

The Pure Bloom relies entirely on the sintered pod leaves for anchoring. No subsurface component is used. The 8 leaves, sintered to the regolith across their full area, create a large bonded foundation that resists lateral and vertical loads.

---

## Advantages

- **No subsurface mechanism required** — eliminates driven anchor complexity and mass
- **Large bonded area** — the sintered bond covers the full leaf area, distributing loads across a wide footprint
- **Robots do the anchoring** — uses existing capability; no new hardware required

---

## Disadvantages and Risks

- **Uplift resistance** — the sintered bond between the leaf underside and the regolith must resist vertical uplift forces. The bond strength of sintered basalt-to-regolith contact has not been measured at the relevant scale.
- **Bond quality uncertainty** — sintering quality across the full leaf area depends on robot execution. Gaps or voids in the sintered bond reduce the effective anchoring area.
- **Regolith bearing** — the leaf foundation spreads load, but if the underlying regolith is weak, the whole foundation can sink or tilt.

---

## Assessment

The Pure Bloom is a strong concept for lateral stability but may be insufficient for uplift resistance under worst-case loading. It is the natural complement to the Rooted Hybrid — if the Rooted Hybrid subsurface anchors cannot be implemented for any reason, the Pure Bloom is the fallback.

Ground testing of sintered leaf-to-simulant bond strength is a high priority for Phase 0.
