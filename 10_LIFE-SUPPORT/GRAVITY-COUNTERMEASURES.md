## The Challenge

The Moon's gravitational acceleration is 1.62 m/s² — approximately 1/6 of Earth's 9.81 m/s². Long-term exposure to this reduced gravity causes measurable physiological deterioration in humans:

- **Bone density loss:** ~1–2% per month in 0g (ISS data). The 1/6g lunar environment will cause slower but still significant loss over years and decades.
- **Muscle atrophy:** Particularly postural muscles — those that normally work against gravity to keep us upright.
- **Cardiovascular deconditioning:** The heart works less hard; over time, cardiac muscle weakens.
- **Fluid redistribution:** Body fluids shift cephalad (toward the head), causing increased intracranial pressure, vision changes, and other effects.

For permanent residents who never return to Earth, the long-term consequences of 1/6g are unknown — ISS data covers months, not decades. CMB must design countermeasures based on the best available evidence while acknowledging this uncertainty honestly.

---

## Three-Layer Countermeasure System

CMB proposes three complementary countermeasures that together aim to provide sufficient mechanical loading to prevent the worst physiological effects:

### Layer 1 — Maglev Sleeping Ring (passive, nightly)
A circular maglev track carrying sleeping pods at ~2.1 rpm at 20-metre radius, generating 1g centrifugal acceleration at the sleeping surface. Residents sleep at 1g every night — 8 hours of full gravitational loading, automatically, without any time or effort cost beyond normal sleep.

See GRAVITY-COUNTERMEASURE-MAGLEV-BEDS.md for full specification.

### Layer 2 — Compression Suit (passive, daytime)
A thin elastic suit applying continuous axial and peripheral compression to the body during waking hours. Based on the Russian Penguin suit concept but updated with modern elastic materials and potentially pneumatic or shape-memory alloy elements. Target: 20–60% of 1g equivalent loading on the skeleton.

The compression suit is worn during normal work and daily activities — no additional time required.

See GRAVITY-COUNTERMEASURE-SUIT.md for full specification (document to be written).

### Layer 3 — Exercise Protocol (active, 1 hour daily)
Structured resistance exercise targeting the muscle groups most affected by low gravity — leg extensors, postural muscles, cardiovascular system. Equipment: resistance machines anchored to the floor (not free weights, which behave differently in 1/6g). Duration: 1 hour per day minimum.

This is the only countermeasure that requires deliberate time and effort from the resident. The other two layers are passive.

---

## Combined Target

| Period | Layer | Duration | G-equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sleep | Maglev ring | 8 hours | 1.0g |
| Waking — wearing suit | Compression suit | ~15 hours | 0.2–0.6g |
| Exercise | Resistance training | 1 hour | Variable, high |

Whether this combination is sufficient to prevent long-term physiological deterioration is an open medical question. It is the best available concept given current knowledge. Monitoring of resident health data over time will either validate the approach or indicate where it needs strengthening.

---

## Research Priority

The medical effectiveness of this combined countermeasure system is the most important health research question for CMB. Collaboration with aerospace medicine researchers — at ESA, NASA, JAXA, universities, and medical institutions — is specifically sought.

All health monitoring data from CMB residents (anonymised and with consent) will be published openly for the benefit of the broader field of space medicine.
