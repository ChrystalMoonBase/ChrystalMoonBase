# Food Production
## Chrystal Moon Base — Life Support

**Status:** Concept proposal  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Overview

Complete nutritional self-sufficiency is the long-term target. Getting there is a gradual process — initial residents are substantially dependent on Earth supply, and local food production increases over time as farm dome area grows and crop systems mature.

---

## Technology Approach — Hydroponics

Soil-based farming is not proposed for the lunar base. Lunar regolith contains no organic matter and would require extensive processing to become a growth medium. Hydroponics — growing plants in nutrient solution — is more appropriate:

- Higher yield per square metre than soil farming
- More water-efficient (closed-loop water recycling)
- No dust or regolith contamination of food products
- Established technology with decades of closed-environment research (Antarctic stations, ISS experiments)

---

## Required Area Estimate

For a nutritionally complete diet, hydroponic farming typically requires 30–50 m² of growing area per person. For 500 residents:

- **Minimum target:** 15,000 m² (multiple large farm domes)
- **Adequate target:** 25,000 m²

This is a very large area. It will not be achieved immediately — farm dome construction proceeds in parallel with all other base construction over years. Initial residents rely substantially on Earth supply, with local production supplementing and gradually replacing imports.

---

## Crop Selection

Criteria: high caloric density per area, fast growing cycle, variety for nutritional completeness and psychological wellbeing.

Candidate crops (based on closed-environment agriculture research):
- **Wheat** — staple carbohydrate
- **Potato** — staple carbohydrate, high yield
- **Soybean** — protein and fat
- **Lettuce and leafy greens** — fast growing, psychological value (fresh food)
- **Tomatoes** — vitamin C, psychological value
- **Spirulina** — extremely high protein and nutrient density per area

No single crop provides complete nutrition. Diversity is essential both nutritionally and psychologically.

---

## Lighting

Farm domes use the natural sunlight transmitted through the dome ceiling, supplemented by artificial lighting as needed. At Peary, the sun is always at a low angle — direct sunlight intensity through the dome ceiling is lower than equatorial conditions but relatively constant. Crops requiring high light intensity may need supplemental LED lighting at additional energy cost.

The energy cost of artificial lighting is a significant factor in the farm dome energy budget — this must be included in the overall power planning.
