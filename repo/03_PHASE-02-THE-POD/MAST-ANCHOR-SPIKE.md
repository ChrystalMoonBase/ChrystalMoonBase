# Mast Anchor — Spike Variant
## Chrystal Moon Base — Phase 0.2 Structural Concept

**Status:** Concept proposal — one of four variants for engineering evaluation  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Concept

The Spike is the simplest anchoring concept. A single central spike — a rigid rod or tube — is driven into the lunar regolith below the pod central column at landing. The mast is mounted on this spike. The pod bloom leaves provide additional lateral stability through contact friction, but are not sintered permanently.

---

## Advantages

- **Simplest mechanism** — one component, one motion
- **Lowest mass** — no additional anchor hardware required beyond the spike itself
- **Proven concept** — driven anchors have been used in terrestrial and Antarctic environments

---

## Disadvantages and Risks

- **Regolith dependence** — spike anchoring capacity depends critically on regolith compaction and cohesion at depth. Peary rim regolith properties are not directly measured. If the regolith is weaker than expected, the spike provides insufficient resistance.
- **No surface bond** — without sintered leaf anchoring, any lateral disturbance (robot activity near the base, dust storm particle impact, micro-seismic events) acts on the spike alone.
- **Non-repairable** — if the spike bends or the regolith around it loosens over time, there is no corrective action available.

---

## Assessment

The Spike is a viable first concept but carries the highest risk of the four variants, due to its complete dependence on subsurface regolith properties that are poorly characterised for the Peary site. It may be appropriate as a secondary anchor combined with surface sintering, but is not recommended as the sole anchoring system.

This assessment is the founder's view. Engineering analysis may reach different conclusions.
