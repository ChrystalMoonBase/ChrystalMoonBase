# Dome Printing Method
## Chrystal Moon Base — Construction Concept

**Status:** Concept proposal — significant engineering development required  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Overview

Domes are not assembled from pre-manufactured components. They are printed in place by CMB-R1 robots from local materials, in a sequence that builds each layer outward from the center, starting with the innermost glass layer and ending with the outer sintered basalt shell.

The "inside-out" printing approach — starting from the inner glass surface and working outward — was chosen because the optical quality of the inner glass surface (which residents will see and which must be smooth and clear) is highest when it is printed first, before the mechanical disturbance of outer layer construction.

---

## Pre-Construction Requirements

Before any dome is printed, the following must be complete:

1. **Sintered basalt foundation ring** — a ring of sintered floor at the dome perimeter, to the exact diameter of the dome. This is the base on which the dome walls will be printed.

2. **Underground connections installed** — all power, data, water, gas, and access connections are routed through the floor and terminated at their connection points inside the dome footprint. All floor penetrations are sealed.

3. **Floor surface prepared** — the floor inside the dome footprint is sintered, levelled, and verified by the Watcher robot.

4. **Printing robots positioned** — sintering robots on the inside of the dome footprint and on the outside simultaneously.

## Printing Sequence

### Step 1 — Inner Glass Layer (Layer 5)
Robots on the inside of the foundation ring begin printing the inner glass layer. The M-Glass module supplies molten glass through a delivery system. Robots lay the glass in molten form, building the dome shell layer by layer from bottom to top.

As the glass is applied, titanium fibre (supplied by M-Titanium) is laid continuously into the molten glass by a second robot arm. The titanium bonds into the glass as it cools, creating the reinforced composite.

The anti-decay coating is applied as a final step on the interior surface before the glass fully cools.

### Step 2 — Water Cavity Structure (Layer 3 outer surface + Layer 4 boundary)
The second glass layer (Layer 3) is printed outside and around Layer 5, with a defined gap between them for the water cavity. A sealing system at the base connects the two glass shells to form a sealed annular cavity.

The gold coating is applied to the exterior surface of Layer 3 before the next layer is applied. Applying it now ensures the gold layer is protected by the subsequent outer layers.

### Step 3 — Self-Sealing Gel Layer (Layer 2)
The self-sealing gel is applied to the exterior of Layer 3. The specific application method depends on the gel formulation selected — spray, extrusion, or injection into a pre-formed cavity are all possible.

### Step 4 — Outer Basalt Shell (Layer 1)
Robots on the outside of the dome begin sintering the outer basalt shell. They work upward from the foundation ring, sintering layer by layer, using local regolith. The dome is built from the outside while the glass is already complete inside.

The outer shell is sintered thick enough for the required radiation shielding. Thickness varies with position — thicker at the top (maximum radiation exposure), potentially thinner on the sides where the underground tunnel connections are made.

### Step 5 — Water Fill
Once the dome is complete and structurally verified, the water cavity (Layer 4) is filled from the underground cistern supply through the floor connection. The fill is gradual — over days or weeks — to allow monitoring for leaks.

### Step 6 — Pressure Test
The dome is pressurised to the design habitat pressure using an automated system. Pressure is held for a defined period and monitored for any drop. Only after successful pressure testing is human access permitted.

## Key Engineering Questions

1. **Glass delivery system** — how is molten glass delivered from the M-Glass module to the printing robot at the dome construction site? What thermal management is required to keep glass molten during transport?
2. **Titanium fibre placement precision** — how does the robot ensure continuous, uniform titanium fibre distribution throughout the glass wall thickness during printing?
3. **Dome height without support** — as the dome grows taller, the unsintered upper sections must support their own weight until complete. What temporary support structure, if any, is required?
4. **Inner and outer coordination** — robots working on the inside and outside of the dome simultaneously must not interfere. What coordination protocol prevents collisions through the growing dome wall?
5. **Quality verification during printing** — how does the Watcher robot verify layer quality at intermediate steps, before subsequent layers are added and make inspection impossible?
