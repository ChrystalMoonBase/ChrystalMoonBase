# Demonstrator 1 — Laser Sintering of Lunar Simulant
## Chrystal Moon Base — Phase 0 Earth Programme

**Status:** Not yet begun  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Objective

Demonstrate that a lunar regolith simulant can be laser-sintered into structurally solid basalt at the energy density achievable with the Phase 0.2 laser system, producing a sample suitable for structural load testing.

---

## Why This Is the First Demonstrator

Laser sintering of lunar simulant is the core physical process of the entire concept. If it does not produce solid, load-bearing material at the required energy input, no other aspect of the design matters.

Published laboratory results (Fateri et al. 2019, Scientific Reports; Taylor et al. various) demonstrate that laser sintering of lunar simulant is possible. However:
- Published tests used small samples (typically a few centimetres)
- Energy densities varied widely between experiments
- Crack formation in larger samples is an unsolved problem
- The specific simulant composition affects results significantly

Demonstrator 1 scales up from laboratory samples to construction-relevant patches and characterises the energy requirements and quality outcomes under controlled conditions.

---

## Test Protocol (Proposed)

**Simulant:** MMS-1 (Marketed Lunar Mare Simulant) or LHS-1 (Lunar Highland Simulant), depending on Peary regolith composition best-match. Both are commercially available.

**Laser:** A commercial ytterbium fibre laser in the 1–5 kW range, operated at wavelengths of approximately 1070 nm (matching the planned flight laser wavelength).

**Test series:**

1. **Energy density sweep** — vary power, scan speed, and spot size to map the parameter space producing crack-free, dense sintered material. Target: identify the minimum energy density for structural quality.

2. **Depth characterisation** — measure sintered depth as a function of energy input. Target: achieve 10–25 cm sintered depth (required for structural foundation).

3. **Area scaling** — produce sintered patches of increasing size (5 cm → 20 cm → 50 cm → 1 m²) to characterise crack formation at scale. Target: crack-free 1 m² patch.

4. **Thermal cycling** — subject sintered samples to thermal cycling representative of the lunar surface environment (−150°C to +130°C, 100 cycles minimum). Target: no crack initiation or propagation under thermal cycling.

5. **Structural testing** — measure compressive strength, tensile strength (Brazilian disc test), and surface hardness of sintered samples. Compare to published data for natural basalt and sintered simulant.

---

## Success Criteria

- Crack-free sintered patches at 1 m² scale
- Sintered depth ≥ 10 cm under achievable energy density
- Compressive strength ≥ 50 MPa (comparable to concrete; natural basalt is typically 100–300 MPa)
- Sample survival through 100 thermal cycles without crack initiation
