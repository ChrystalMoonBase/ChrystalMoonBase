## Overview

This document summarises published illumination measurements and models for the Peary Crater rim region. All data is from third-party missions and publications. CMB has not conducted any original measurements.

---

## Published Illumination Fractions

| Source | Method | Peary Rim Peak Illumination |
|---|---|---|
| Bussey et al. 2005 (Clementine) | Optical imaging | >80% for best peaks |
| Noda et al. 2008 (SELENE/Kaguya) | Laser altimeter topography + modelling | ~89% for best peaks |
| Mazarico et al. 2011 (LRO) | LOLA altimeter + solar model | 86–94% depending on peak |

The ~89% figure used in CMB documents is based on the Noda et al. (2008) SELENE data, which is frequently cited as the most comprehensive pre-LRO illumination study of the Peary rim.

---

## What These Numbers Mean in Practice

89% annual illumination at Peary means:
- ~326 days per year with sunlight at the measurement point
- ~39 days per year in shadow (distributed across the year in short periods)
- The longest single shadow period at the best peaks is typically a few days
- No continuous 14-day lunar night as experienced at the equator

For a solar-powered system, this means approximately **11% downtime** must be managed by battery storage and hibernation protocols.

---

## Spatial Resolution Limitations

Published illumination data has spatial resolution of approximately:
- Clementine (Bussey 2005): ~70 metre pixels
- SELENE/Kaguya (Noda 2008): ~10–50 metre resolution from altimeter
- LRO LOLA (Mazarico 2011): ~10–20 metre resolution

At these resolutions, individual flat zones of 20–30 metres may not be fully resolved. A 10-metre landing zone could have significantly different illumination than the surrounding area due to local topographic shadowing that the published models cannot resolve.

**Implication:** Published illumination data provides a strong basis for site region selection, but is insufficient for precise landing zone selection. A dedicated high-resolution orbital observation campaign is required before flight.
