# CMB Authority — Scope and Limits
## Chrystal Moon Base — Governance

**Status:** Founding concept document  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## What CMB Has Authority Over

CMB holds authority over all base-wide infrastructure and safety systems. This authority is not negotiable and cannot be overridden by any tenant, regardless of their resources or political status.

**CMB-exclusive domains:**
- Safety system operation and maintenance
- Underground network — all tunnels, utility conduits, access systems
- Power grid — generation, distribution, allocation
- Atmospheric management for the base as a whole
- Water distribution network
- AI monitoring system operation
- Surface access facilities (arrival and departure pads)
- All CMB-R1 robots
- The mast and laser power system

In these domains, CMB decisions are final. If a safety decision conflicts with a tenant's operational preferences, the safety decision takes precedence. Always.

---

## What CMB Does NOT Have Authority Over

Within tenant-leased dome clusters, tenants govern themselves. CMB does not:
- Direct tenant research programmes
- Control tenant internal governance
- Dictate tenant working schedules
- Override tenant decisions on non-safety matters within their domes

The boundary is: if it only affects the tenant's own domes and does not involve shared infrastructure, it is the tenant's decision.

---

## The Safety Override

If any action by any tenant — deliberate or accidental — threatens base-wide safety, CMB has the authority and the obligation to intervene immediately. The AI monitoring system will detect the threat. The duty engineer will act.

This override capability is disclosed to all tenants before their lease agreement is signed. It is not a surprise.

---

## Evolving Authority

CMB authority as described here is appropriate for the founding phases when CMB is the sole builder and operator of the base. As the base grows, as more tenants arrive, and as a resident community forms, the governance structure should evolve toward greater representation of those who live and work there.

The specific form of that evolution — elected councils, tenant representatives, resident assemblies — is not defined here. It is a question for the people who will live on the base, informed by the experience of operating it.

CMB commits to not opposing this evolution. The goal is a base that serves its residents and all of humanity — not a perpetual founder autocracy.
