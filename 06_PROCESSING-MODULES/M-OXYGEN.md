## Overview

**Input:** Lunar regolith (any composition) — all major lunar minerals contain oxygen

**Output:** Gaseous and liquid oxygen

**Process:** Molten oxide electrolysis (MOE) has been demonstrated as the most promising process for lunar oxygen production. Regolith is melted and an electrical current is passed through the melt, driving oxygen to the anode and metal alloy to the cathode. ESA and MIT have demonstrated this process in laboratory conditions. NASA's MOXIE experiment on Mars Perseverance demonstrated CO₂-based oxygen production at TRL 7 — the lunar process is different but the principle of in-situ oxygen production is validated at mission scale.

**Why important:** Oxygen is the habitat atmosphere component and rocket oxidiser. It is needed before humans arrive. However, its production does not need to begin before glass and titanium — the dome construction sequence means habitable pressure vessels do not exist until Phase 1.3 at the earliest.

**Key challenge:** MOE requires stable high-temperature melt containment over long operating periods. Crucible materials that survive the corrosive molten regolith environment are a specific engineering challenge.
