## Why the South Pole Is a Strong Alternative

The south polar region of the Moon has received significant attention from space agencies worldwide. Artemis III targets the south pole. India's Chandrayaan-3 landed near the south pole in 2023. NASA's VIPER rover was planned for the south pole. This concentration of interest is not arbitrary — the south pole has real advantages.

This document explains why CMB currently proposes Peary (north pole) rather than the south pole, while acknowledging that the south pole remains a valid and arguably better-resourced alternative.

---

## South Pole Advantages

**Water ice:** The south polar cold traps — particularly Haworth, Nobile, Shackleton, and the de Gerlache–Shackleton ridge — have been studied more extensively than north polar equivalents. LCROSS impact data, M3 spectral data, and ShadowCam imaging provide better characterisation of south polar ice than is currently available for Peary.

**International infrastructure:** Multiple missions are planning south polar operations. Landing infrastructure, communications relay satellites, and surface operations experience will accumulate faster at the south pole than the north. A CMB base at the south pole would benefit from this investment by others.

**Political visibility:** The Artemis Accords and associated missions have made the south pole the focus of the current generation of lunar exploration. Institutional partners considering CMB collaboration may find a south polar location easier to support.

---

## Why CMB Currently Proposes North Peary

**Topographic complexity:** The south pole sits within the South Pole-Aitken basin, a 2,500 km impact structure with 16 km of altitude variation. The terrain around the illuminated peaks at the south pole — particularly the Shackleton-de Gerlache ridge — is more complex and steeper than the Peary rim. This increases landing risk and construction difficulty.

**Illumination:** Current data suggests the best south polar illumination sites achieve 70–85% annual illumination — somewhat lower than Peary's estimated 89%. This difference is meaningful for a solar-powered system.

**Crowding:** The south pole will be a busy place. Multiple nations and companies are planning operations there. CMB proposes a different approach — a permanent open base, not a national outpost. Operating at a less contested location may be practically easier.

---

## The Honest Assessment

The north-south choice matters less than the quality of engineering on whichever site is chosen. CMB technology is designed to be location-agnostic within polar constraints. If future data, international cooperation, or regulatory factors favour the south pole, the technical concept adapts without fundamental redesign.

The south pole should not be dismissed. If an institutional partner with south polar expertise approaches CMB, the south pole becomes the preferred site.

This is an open question. Input from polar lunar geologists and mission planners is specifically requested.
