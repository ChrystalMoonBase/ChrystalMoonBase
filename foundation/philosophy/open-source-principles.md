## Why Open Source

Every major advance in human knowledge has come from people building on the work of others. The scientific method itself is open source: you publish your method, your data, and your results, and you invite the world to find your errors.

Space exploration has largely not worked this way. Designs are proprietary. Data is classified. Patents are filed. Nations compete rather than collaborate.

Chrystal Moon Base proposes a different approach — not because open source is ideologically pure, but because it is practically superior for this specific problem.

Building a permanent human presence on the Moon is too large, too complex, and too important to be owned by any single organisation. It requires contributions from thousands of people across dozens of disciplines over multiple generations. No company, no nation, and no individual — including the founder of this project — is capable of doing it alone.

Open source is not a gesture. It is the only realistic way to build something this large.

## What Open Source Means Here

**All design documents are public.**  
Every concept, every calculation, every engineering sketch produced under the Chrystal Moon Base name is published in this repository under open licence. There are no private versions. There are no documents held back for competitive reasons.

**All errors are public.**  
When a number is wrong, that correction is published. When an assumption fails, that failure is documented. The history of mistakes is as valuable as the history of progress.

**All contributors receive credit.**  
Every person who improves a document, corrects a calculation, runs a simulation, or contributes in any way is named in CREDITS.md. Contributions are not absorbed anonymously into the project — they belong to the people who made them.

**No patents will be filed.**  
Any technology developed under this project will not be patented. It will be published openly so that any organisation — commercial, governmental, or academic — can use it, improve it, and build on it without permission or payment.

**The Moon is not for sale.**  
No part of the lunar surface, no resource extracted from it, and no infrastructure built on it under this project will be claimed as private property. The Outer Space Treaty (1967) Article II is the legal foundation. CMB interprets it as: the Moon belongs to all of humanity, and therefore everything built to reach it should serve all of humanity.

## What Open Source Does Not Mean

Open source does not mean unstructured. This repository has standards for documentation, methods for contribution, and a review process for changes. See CONTRIBUTING.md.

Open source does not mean leaderless. During the founding phase, Berechja Kerkdijk holds final authority on the direction of the project. This will evolve as the project grows and institutional partnerships form. See GOVERNANCE.md.

Open source does not mean that everything is equally valid. Corrections to technical documents must be supported by published data or verifiable calculation. Opinion without evidence is welcome as a discussion — it does not replace documented fact.

## The Linux Principle

The Linux operating system was built by thousands of contributors working independently on small, modular pieces. No single person understood the whole system. The result is infrastructure that runs most of the world's servers, most smartphones, and most spacecraft.

This repository is organised on the same principle. Each document is a small, independent module. An engineer who knows nothing about robot locomotion can contribute to dome construction. A materials scientist can improve a single processing module document without needing to understand the power budget. A student can correct a unit conversion error.

Small contributions add up. They always have.

## An Honest Note

This project was started by one person with no engineering degree, no laboratory, and no institutional support. The documents in this repository are concept proposals, not engineering specifications. Every number is an estimate. Every design is a starting point.

The purpose of publishing this openly is precisely so that people who do have the expertise can engage with it — to improve it, to correct it, or to demonstrate clearly why something cannot work.

If this project contributes nothing else to the field, it will have contributed a set of openly available questions. That is enough to justify publishing it.
