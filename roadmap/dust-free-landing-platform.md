# Dust-Free Landing Platform (Later Phase — Parked)

**Status:** Parked concept — **NOT part of the first base.** The first base lands on

## The problem this would solve

Landing a rocket on bare lunar regolith blasts the surroundings with high-speed
ejecta. Because the Moon has effectively no atmosphere, the particles are not slowed
by air — their motion is **nearly ballistic**, so they travel far and hit hard.

Sourced facts:
- During the Apollo 12 landing, the **Surveyor 3 craft ~155–180 m away was scoured
  and pitted with hundreds of tiny holes** by the ejected regolith.
- Plume ejecta velocities have been estimated in the range of **~300–2,000 m/s** —
  close to the lunar escape velocity (~2,373 m/s).
- A single human-class lander can **eject on the order of 100 t of regolith** during
  descent, with a blast zone on the order of **~1 km radius**.
- The exhaust also **injects plume chemicals (including unburnt fuel) into the
  regolith**, which **may freeze into nearby cold traps**.

So: bare-regolith landings sandblast everything nearby, dig a crater under the
lander, and can contaminate cold-trap ice.

---

## Why it matters more at Peary specifically

- **The ice.** Peary's value is its cold-trap ice (if confirmed). A plume near those
  traps could inject fuel chemicals into the very ice you want to mine. A controlled
  pad — and landing well away from the ice — protects the resource.
- **Polar soil may be more porous.** Preliminary data suggests polar regolith is
  highly porous (≈70% to a few metres depth), which makes bearing-capacity failure
  and deep cratering on landing **more** likely than at the Apollo sites.

## The concept (to be developed)

A prepared, **dust-free landing platform**: a sintered/paved pad (and possibly
surrounding berms or blast walls) that eliminates loose-dust sandblasting once
landing traffic grows. Built by the same robots that sinter the base floor.

This is the natural home for the **titanium-as-construction-metal** question.
Titanium is *not* needed as dome reinforcement (alumina replaces it), but a
launch/landing structure with heat-loaded, high-strength parts is exactly where
titanium's toughness and high-temperature strength are justified — see
`mare-production-base.md` for the import-vs-local-source question.

---

## Why it is parked (not first-base)

Every lunar landing so far has succeeded on bare regolith. The first base does the
same: land on the surface, no platform, no titanium structure, no mare. The
dust-free platform becomes worth building only **after** the base exists and traffic
(and the need to protect the ice and hardware) increases.
