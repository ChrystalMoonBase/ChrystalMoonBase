## The Moment Everything Changes

When the base begins exporting products to Earth, it crosses a fundamental threshold — from a facility that costs money to one that generates it. This is the point at which the mission becomes self-sustaining.

Everything before this point is investment. Everything after is momentum.

## What the Moon Can Export That Earth Cannot Match

The Moon provides manufacturing conditions that are impossible or prohibitively expensive to replicate on Earth:

**Perfect vacuum — free:**
Processes that require vacuum chambers on Earth — thin-film deposition, certain metallurgical processes, crystal growth — happen naturally on the lunar surface. No vacuum pump. No chamber. No energy cost for maintaining vacuum.

**Microgravity-adjacent processes:**
1/6 gravity enables crystal growth, protein crystallisation, and material solidification with properties that Earth's full gravity disrupts. Pharmaceutical crystals grown in low gravity may have superior properties for drug delivery. Optical crystals grown without gravitational stress may have superior clarity.

**Extreme temperature differential — free:**
From −150°C in shadow to +130°C in sunlight, with no atmosphere to complicate heat management. Processes requiring extreme thermal cycling are straightforward on the Moon.

**No atmosphere — no contamination:**
Certain material purification processes are contaminated by atmospheric gases on Earth. In lunar vacuum, they are not.

## Priority Export Products

### Helium-3
Implanted in lunar regolith by billions of years of solar wind. Extremely rare on Earth. Potential fusion fuel — if and when commercial fusion reactors make He-3 fusion practical. The M-He3 processing module extracts it from bulk regolith.

The value of He-3 depends entirely on the development of fusion technology. This is a speculative long-term export — included because the resource exists and because the base will be operating long enough for fusion to potentially mature.

### Ultra-Pure Materials
Silicon, titanium, and aluminium processed in lunar vacuum achieve purity levels that are extremely costly to achieve on Earth. High-purity silicon for advanced semiconductors. Titanium for aerospace and medical applications. The lunar processing advantage is real and potentially commercially significant.

### Specialty Crystals and Optical Components
Crystals grown in 1/6g lunar vacuum may have properties unachievable on Earth — superior optical clarity, more perfect lattice structure, better performance in specific applications. The pharmaceutical and optics industries are potential customers.

### Scientific Samples
Processed lunar regolith, subsurface samples from Watcher robot drilling, ice samples from cold trap extraction — all have scientific value to research institutions on Earth that cannot send their own missions.

## The Export Logistics

Rockets returning to Earth carry payload. An empty returning rocket is a wasted opportunity. Export products are sized and packaged to fit within returning vehicle payload capacity — every flight back to Earth carries something of value.

The longer-term question of whether a mass driver, space elevator, or other export infrastructure could reduce costs significantly is left entirely to engineers and physicists. The founder acknowledges this goes beyond current understanding. What is certain: if the products have sufficient value, the logistics will be solved.

## Revenue Structure

**The BV handles all commercial operations:**
- Livestream subscriptions and pay-per-view
- Export product sales
- Infrastructure lease income from tenants
- Licensing of manufacturing capacity to Earth companies

**Revenue flows to the Stichting:**
All BV profits — after operational costs and market-rate founder compensation — flow to Stichting Chrystal Base. The Stichting uses these funds exclusively for mission expansion: new pods, base extensions, new projects.

**The Stichting never accumulates wealth:**
Revenue is reinvested continuously. Pod 2, Pod 3, Pod 4. Mars eventually. The mission does not end — it expands.

## The Livestream as Revenue Engine

From the first sintering ring of Pod 1, the world watches. A paid livestream accessible globally shows the base being built and lived in. Every milestone — first robot, first sintered ring, first dome, first human landing — is a global event with commercial value.

The livestream does several things simultaneously:
- Generates revenue for the BV → flows to Stichting → funds next pod
- Creates global awareness and support for the mission
- Provides transparency — anyone can watch what is being built and how
- Builds the community of people who care about this project before the base exists

This is not marketing. It is the open source philosophy applied to visibility — show everything, hide nothing, let the world decide if it matters.
