## Why this exists

Peary is a highland (anorthosite) site: rich in aluminium, silicon, calcium, oxygen
— and near-permanent sunlight and possible polar ice. But it is **poor in iron and
titanium** (no ilmenite). The lunar **mare** plains have the opposite profile:
iron- and titanium-bearing (ilmenite), but no peaks of eternal light and no polar
cold traps.

That is not a contradiction — it is a possible **division of labour between two
locations**: Peary = the home base (power, ice, living, aluminium/glass); a mare
site = raw-material extraction for iron and titanium.

---

## What it would actually be for

- **Titanium and iron** — the two things Peary lacks. Note: after analysis,
  titanium is *not* needed as dome reinforcement (alumina wins). Its real use is as
  a **construction metal for landers, launch/landing structures, engines and
  heat-loaded parts** (see `dust-free-landing-platform.md`). Whether that justifies
  a whole second base — versus simply importing titanium from Earth — is the central
  open question.

---

## Why it is parked (not first-base)

A mare base is, in effect, a **second base**: its own power, maintenance, robots,
and supply chain. It is far away (hundreds to thousands of km from Peary), so it
adds a hard logistics problem. For the first base, **importing titanium from Earth
is simpler and cheaper**, and — because the first base lands on bare regolith and
needs no titanium launch/landing structure — the first base needs neither mare nor
titanium at all.

## The transport problem (open — your "4 pods" concern)

Moving material between a mare site and Peary would likely use a maglev line. Maglev
is near-frictionless (vacuum, levitation), so *holding a load at speed* costs almost
nothing. The real costs are:

- **Acceleration** to cruise speed needs high peak power; several loaded pods at
  once multiply that peak.
- **Braking / no trackside grid:** over hundreds of km there is no power network en
  route, so energy must be carried on board or supplied by solar/relay stations
  along the line. (On Earth you regenerate braking energy into the grid; here there
  is no grid to feed.)

So the intuition that "4 pods probably can't all be pulled with energy at once" is
**well-founded** — not because of levitation, but because of acceleration peak power
and the absence of a trackside grid. Likely directions: fewer pods at once, lighter
loads, or a phased route with solar/charging stations. **This is engineering
analysis for later — not decided here.**
