# Sleep Habitat Dome
## Chrystal Moon Base — Habitat Concept

**Status:** Concept proposal  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Overview

The sleep habitat is the smallest dome type — home to 6–10 permanent residents. It is the fundamental unit of private life on the base. The deliberate small size is the most important design decision: if this dome fails catastrophically, the maximum number of people affected is 10. The remaining residents are safe.

---

## Dimensions (Estimated)

- **Diameter:** 10–15 metres
- **Interior height at apex:** 5–7 metres
- **Floor area:** approximately 80–175 m²

For 10 residents, this provides approximately 8–17 m² per person of floor area — comparable to a small studio apartment. Given that the dome is used primarily for sleeping and personal time (with communal activities in larger domes), this is considered a minimum acceptable standard.

---

## Interior Layout (Concept)

The interior is not pre-defined — residents and their tenant organisation customise their space within the dome. A reference layout might include:

- Individual sleeping pods or cabins along the perimeter wall — 6–10 units
- A small communal area in the center
- Hygiene facilities accessible from below (water and waste routed underground)
- Personal storage for each resident
- Emergency equipment cabinet (sealed, accessible without airlock)

The floor hatch to the underground network is the only entrance. It includes a small airlock chamber between the tunnel level and the dome interior, preventing atmospheric mixing during access.

---

## The Privacy Boundary

The sleep habitat dome is the only space on the entire base where AI monitoring does not apply inside the dome. This is the one private space — where the AI watches the health of the dome (pressure, temperature, atmosphere composition) but does not monitor individual behaviour.

This boundary is absolute and is guaranteed in the base governance charter.

Outside the dome — in the underground network, in communal domes, in all shared spaces — monitoring is continuous and non-negotiable for safety reasons.

---

## Looking Up

From inside a sleep habitat dome, looking up through the inner glass layer, through the water, through the gold-tinted outer glass — the lunar sky is visible. Stars during the long lunar night. The slowly moving sun filtered to safe levels during the lunar day.

This view is not incidental. Long-duration habitation in enclosed spaces without natural light causes significant psychological stress. The transparent dome ceiling provides the visual connection to the exterior environment that residents need — filtered, protected, but present.
