# Underground Network — Overview
## Chrystal Moon Base — Infrastructure Concept

**Status:** Concept proposal 
**Author:** Berechja Kerkdijk 
**Licence:** CC0

## The Fundamental Principle

Dust is the enemy. Everything that moves, everything that connects, everything that transfers between locations — goes underground.

The dome is strong because it has no penetrations. The base is safe because nothing critical is exposed to the lunar surface. The underground network is what makes both of these things possible. It is not a support system for the base — it is the circulatory system of the base. The domes are organs sitting on top of it.

---

## What Goes Underground

**Everything.**

- All human movement between domes
- All cargo transport between facilities
- All robot movement during base operations
- All utilities — power, water, gas, data, laser
- All maglev transport
- All connections to the arrival and departure platforms
- All processing module interconnections
- Emergency evacuation routes

Nothing of operational significance moves on the surface of the 20-hectare floor after the base is built. The surface belongs to the domes and to the robots that maintain the exterior.

---

## The 20-Hectare Sintered Floor

The entire base sits on a single continuous sintered basalt floor of approximately 20 hectares — 505 metres in diameter. This is not divided into sections or phases. It is one unbroken surface.

**Why one continuous floor:**
- No seams, no joints, no transitions between sections
- No weak points where dust can infiltrate between plates
- Structurally far stronger than a segmented approach
- The underground network below it is enclosed by one continuous roof

The floor is simultaneously the foundation of everything above it and the ceiling of everything below it.

**Construction sequence:**
1. Robots create a sintered basalt path from the Pod to the centre of the planned floor area
2. From the centre, sintering expands outward in concentric rings
3. The underground tunnel network is sintered simultaneously — tunnels are built into the floor as it is constructed, not excavated afterward
4. All utility conduits are installed during tunnel construction before the floor above is completed
5. Only when the full floor and tunnel network are complete do dome foundations begin

## Human Movement — Walking, Not Maglev

Within the 20-hectare base, people walk. The maximum distance across the base is 505 metres — a comfortable 5-minute walk. Maglev is not needed and not used for internal base movement.

The underground walking network connects all domes via a system of passages wide enough for comfortable two-way pedestrian traffic. These passages are designed as living spaces, not service corridors.

**Passage design:**
- Height: minimum 2.2 metres — comfortable for tall people
- Width: sufficient for two people to pass comfortably, wider at junctions
- Lighting: luminescent algae pillars — living light sources distributed throughout all passages. Never dark, never harsh artificial lighting. The same algae that process atmosphere in the domes line the walls of the passages, providing soft blue-green organic light that also contributes to CO₂ removal and O₂ production throughout the network
- Atmosphere: the passages are part of the base atmospheric system — the algae pillars are functional, not decorative
- Orientation: clear visual landmarks at all junctions — the algae pillar density and colour variants help residents navigate intuitively

**The Waldorf principle:** the passages are designed as pleasant, human spaces. People walk through them many times every day. A beautiful, living environment in the passages contributes to psychological health, reduces stress, and supports the social cohesion of a community living in an isolated environment. This is not a luxury — it is a design requirement for long-duration habitation.

## Maglev — Long-Distance Transport Only

The maglev system serves connections that are too far to walk:

**Within the base:**
The maglev does not operate within the 20-hectare floor area. Walking is the transport mode inside the base.

**Base to arrival platform:**
The platform where incoming spacecraft land is separated from the habitat area — rockets are violent, their exhaust contaminates everything within range. A maglev tunnel connects the base underground network to the arrival platform. Multiple decontamination stages are integrated into this tunnel before any person or cargo enters the base network.

**Base to departure platform:**
A separate maglev tunnel connects to the departure platform. Arrival and departure flows are never mixed — a dedicated unidirectional flow in each direction.

**Future inter-base connections:**
As the lunar civilisation grows — additional bases at other Peary peaks, eventually the south pole and equatorial spaceport — the maglev network extends to connect them. Inter-base maglev operates at much higher speeds than the platform connections, taking advantage of the vacuum environment to reach speeds impractical on Earth.

## Decontamination — The Border Between Outside and Inside

Every point where the outside world connects to the inside base network is a decontamination boundary. Nothing crosses this boundary without passing through the full decontamination protocol.

**Locations with decontamination:**
- Arrival platform maglev terminal — maximum protocol, multiple stages
- Departure platform — simplified outbound protocol
- Any future surface access point

**The protocol exists because:**
Lunar dust is electrostatically charged, chemically reactive, and physically sharp. One contaminated person or container can introduce dust into a network designed to be completely dust-free. The inside of the base is maintained at cleanroom-equivalent standards. The decontamination boundary maintains that standard absolutely.

See DECONTAMINATION-SEQUENCE. md for the full protocol.

## Safety — Underground as Last Resort

The underground network is not where people live. People live in the domes — with light, transparent ceilings, views of the lunar sky, and the psychological benefits of open space and natural light.

The underground network is where people go when a dome fails.

**Emergency protocol:**
If any dome loses pressure or is otherwise compromised, residents evacuate immediately through the floor hatch into the underground network. The network maintains its own independent atmospheric integrity — a dome failure does not affect the underground passages. Residents are safe below while the dome situation is assessed and managed.

This is the reason the underground network is built to human comfort standards rather than pure utility — it must function as a survivable emergency space as well as a daily transport network. But it is always the emergency option, never the primary living space.

## Robot Access

CMB-R1 robots access the underground network for:
- Moving processing module outputs between facilities
- Maintenance of underground utility systems
- Responding to alerts in restricted utility zones
- Carrying cargo from the maglev terminal to destination domes

Robot passages are separate from human passages at most points — robots are large, carry heavy loads, and move at speeds incompatible with pedestrian traffic. Junction points exist where robot and human passages intersect, with clear priority protocols managed by the AI monitoring system.

---

## Utility Conduits

All utilities run through dedicated conduit channels integrated into the tunnel walls during construction:

- **Electrical power** — high-voltage DC distribution from the surface power grid to all domes and facilities
- **Data and laser** — fibre optic and laser communication links
- **Water** — supply and return for all dome water systems including dome water shield layers
- **Gas** — oxygen, nitrogen, and other atmospheric components
- **Waste** — return lines from all domes to processing facilities

All conduits are accessible for maintenance via dedicated access points at regular intervals. Routine maintenance is performed by robots. Human access to utility conduits requires authorisation and is logged by the AI monitoring system.

## Open Questions

1. What tunnel cross-section dimensions optimally serve both human pedestrian traffic and robot cargo transport, while remaining structurally achievable in sintered basalt?
2. What depth below the surface floor provides adequate radiation shielding and thermal stability for the tunnel environment?
3. What specific algae species and pillar design provide the optimal combination of light output, CO₂ absorption, O₂ production, and growth management in a sealed tunnel environment?
4. What is the structural span limit for sintered basalt tunnel sections without additional support, and how does this constrain junction design?
5. How is the pressure integrity of the tunnel network maintained if a section is breached — what isolation mechanisms prevent a single breach from depressurising the entire network?
