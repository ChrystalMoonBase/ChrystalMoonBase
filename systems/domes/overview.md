## Chrystal Moon Base — Habitat System

## The Dome as the Basic Unit of Habitation

Every pressurised space on the Chrystal Moon Base is a dome. There are no corridors connecting domes at ground level. There are no windows. There are no doors in the dome walls. Every dome is a sealed, self-contained pressure vessel sitting on the sintered basalt floor, connected to everything else exclusively through the underground network.

This design is not an aesthetic choice. It is the logical result of three requirements taken seriously simultaneously:

1. **Structural integrity** — a dome with no wall penetrations is structurally stronger than one with holes, fittings, and frames
2. **Pressure safety** — a sealed dome that can only fail at its floor connection fails in a controllable, containable way
3. **Radiation protection** — the continuous, unbroken layer structure is only possible if there are no penetrations

---

## Scale and Variety

The total planned dome count for a fully operational Phase 2 base is approximately **90–100 domes** of varying sizes. This large number is a deliberate safety decision: many small domes, each housing a limited number of people or a specific function, mean that any single dome failure is survivable. A dome breach that affects 6–10 sleeping residents is a serious emergency. A dome breach that affects 500 people is a catastrophe.

**Dome types and functions:**
- Sleep habitats (6–10 people per dome)
- Farm domes
- Medical domes
- Factory/manufacturing domes
- Research domes
- Entertainment and social domes
- Command and communications domes
- Storage domes
- Processing module enclosures

---

## No Penetrations — The Fundamental Rule

This cannot be overstated: **no cable, pipe, person, or object enters or exits a dome through its wall or roof.** Everything goes through the floor and underground.

The practical implications:
- All utility connections (power, water, gas, data) are routed into the dome through sealed floor penetrations before the dome is printed
- All human access is through floor hatches connecting to the underground transport network
- All cargo arrives from below
- All waste leaves below
- Robots enter and exit through floor hatches

The floor penetrations — which are unavoidable — are engineered as the strongest and most reliable elements of the whole dome. They are the only weak points, so they receive the most engineering attention.

## Construction Sequence

See DOME-PRINTING-METHOD.md for the full construction sequence. In brief:

1. Sintered basalt floor prepared by robots
2. All underground connections installed and sealed
3. Outer basalt shell printed by robots (inside-out)
4. Glass layers printed simultaneously from inside
5. Titanium fibre woven into molten glass during printing
6. Water cavity sealed and filled
7. Pressure test before any human access
