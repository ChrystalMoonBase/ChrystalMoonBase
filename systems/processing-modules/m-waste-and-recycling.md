# M-Waste & Recycling — The Near-Closed Loop

**Status:** Concept proposal, sourced. Tentative where noted.
**Licence:** CC0
**Role:** The connective tissue of the whole factory landscape: where every "waste"
stream goes. The guiding finding — **there is almost no true waste; most residues are
feedstock for something else.**

> **Founding principle (from the founder):** *If we ever begin something this big, we
> do everything — however hard. It is a learning process and a challenge. The
> difficulty is not a reason to discard a stream; the difficulty is the project.*

On a world with no resupply, "throwing away" is barely an option: every kilogram mined
or lifted is worth keeping. Re-utilising residues to **close the material circle** is
the basis for a zero-waste, circular economy on the Moon, and it directly reduces
dependence on Earth.

---

## The residue streams and where they go

### 1. Ceramic slag (from M-MRE) — the biggest residue
- **Reuse:** directly as **construction material** and, notably, as **radiation
  shielding** — spent/processed solids are *enriched in heavier elements* and are
  therefore **more effective shielding than unprocessed regolith**, with glassy
  content acting as a binder for stronger sintered products. Alternatively, slag can be
  **returned to the electrolysis cell and re-electrolysed** to more oxygen and metals.
- **Honest caveat:** if *not* used, slag is the one stream that could pile up and
  overwhelm storage. So slag handling must be designed in, not assumed away.

### 2. Impure ingot ends (from silicon SF-3/SF-4 and metal separation)
- **Reuse:** the cut-off, impurity-rich tail goes **back to M-MRE** as feed. The
  impurities re-enter the system; nothing is lost — a closed sub-loop.

### 3. Anode sludge / contaminant metals (from electrorefining)
- **Reuse:** the Cr, Mn, Ni, Mo (reactor contaminants) and any Cu are **valuable
  alloying metals you have no other local source for.** Capture them — this "waste" is
  effectively your only local supply of those elements.

### 4. Boron-loaded slag (from silicon SF-2)
- **Reuse:** store, or recover; worst case it is inert slag used as construction fill.
  The boron itself is a tiny mass and is not needed for anything.

### 5. Volatile condensates (Mg, Ca, P from distillation/vacuum melt)
- **Reuse:** what evaporates is **condensed and captured as product** (Mg, Ca metals).
  Phosphorus is a small, unneeded stream — sequestered.

### 6. Water-extraction residues (if ice is processed)
- **Reuse:** dried regolith → back to the build line / shielding. Small volatiles
  (ammonia, H₂S, CO₂) → **C / N / S feedstock** for atmosphere and agriculture.

### 7. Fine / unprocessable regolith
- **Reuse:** the simplest reuse of all — **piled as loose radiation-shielding and
  thermal-insulation mass** over habitats. You need bulk shielding mass anyway, so
  "waste" fines have an immediate home.

### 8. Waste heat
- **Reuse:** hot spent solids leave reactors much hotter than mined regolith; that heat
  can be used as **preheat** for sintering/pressing — recycling energy, not just matter.

## Consumables — the real waste to guard against

The streams above are matter that merely *changes factory*. The genuine waste risk is
the **Earth-sourced consumables** that degrade: fluoride electrolytes, the geopolymer
alkali activator, any imported salt. These are precisely what the design tries to
**minimise and keep in a near-closed, recycled loop** — reserved as recycled reagents,
never consumed as a one-shot product. (This is why the purification factories each
carry an Earth-dependence line.)

---

## The honest nuance — a loop is not free

"Almost no waste" does **not** mean "effortless." Reusing a stream costs **storage,
transport and energy**: slag must be moved, ingot ends carried back, heat captured in
time. The substances do not run out or vanish — but the loop is a *deliberately
engineered* one, with real handling cost. The point is not that recycling is automatic;
it is that, with no resupply, building the loop is worth the difficulty — which is the
founding principle of this document.

## Conceptual schematic — the near-closed loop

```
        regolith ──► BUILD LINE ──► structure
            │             ▲
            ▼             │ slag / fines / spent solids → shielding & fill
          M-MRE ──► O₂ + metals + slag
            ▲   │            │
   ingot ends   │            └─► slag → re-electrolyse  OR  shielding
   & impurity   ▼
   tails ◄── PURIFICATION ──► pure products
                  │
                  └─► anode sludge (Cr/Mn/Ni/Mo/Cu) → captured as alloy metals
        consumables (salt/activator) ──► recycled in near-closed loop ──┐
                                  ▲────────────────────────────────────┘
```
*Conceptual; storage/transport/energy costs of each return path are open parameters.*
