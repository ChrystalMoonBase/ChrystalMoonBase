# Processing Modules — Overview
## Chrystal Moon Base — Resource Extraction System

**Status:** Concept proposal  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Purpose

The Moon contains every material needed to build a city. Iron, aluminium, silicon, titanium, oxygen, water, glass — all present in the lunar regolith and rocks in quantities sufficient for large-scale construction. The processing modules are the factories that extract these materials from the Moon and convert them into usable forms.

Each module is an independent, self-contained processing unit. It has a defined input (regolith, or the output of another module), a defined output (processed material), and a defined energy requirement. Modules are designed to be delivered to the Moon as compact units, moved to their operational position by CMB-R1 robots, connected to the base power and utility network, and operated autonomously.

---

## Priority Order

The modules are not all needed at the same time. The priority order reflects the construction sequence:

**First priority — construction materials (before humans arrive):**
1. **M-Glass** — domes cannot be built without glass
2. **M-Titanium** — dome structural reinforcement and pressure vessels
3. **M-Basalt** — structural material, already partially produced by sintering robots
4. **M-Aluminium** — secondary structures and connectors

**Second priority — life support preparation (before humans arrive):**
5. **M-Oxygen** — habitat atmosphere
6. **M-Water** — life support and dome water layer
7. **M-Nitrogen** — atmosphere buffer gas

**Third priority — capability expansion (concurrent with first human habitation):**
8. **M-Iron** — heavy construction, machinery
9. **M-Silicon** — electronics, photovoltaics
10. **M-Fuel** — propellant production for departure rockets
11. **M-Cement** — alternative structural binder

**Long-term:**
12. **M-Print** — advanced additive manufacturing
13. **M-Helium3** — fusion fuel (speculative, very long-term)

**The ideal scenario:** All 13 modules arrive together on a single delivery mission, as compact as possible. Robots move them to their operational positions simultaneously. This minimises transit missions and allows all production to begin in parallel.

## Module Design Principles

All modules share these design constraints:
- **Mass:** As low as possible within functional requirements. Must fit in a Falcon Heavy or equivalent payload
- **Connections:** Standardised interfaces for power (electrical), feedstock input (pipe or conveyor), and product output (storage or pipe)
- **Autonomy:** All modules operate autonomously after initial setup. No human operators required
- **Scalability:** Multiple identical modules can be connected in parallel to increase output
- **Failure isolation:** A failed module does not stop the other modules
