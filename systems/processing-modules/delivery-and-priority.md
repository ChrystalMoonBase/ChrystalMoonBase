## The Ideal Scenario

All 13 processing modules arrive together on a single delivery mission. They are compact — the smallest and simplest possible unit capable of their function. Robots move them from the landing site to their operational positions simultaneously. All production begins in parallel.

This is the ideal. Whether it is achievable depends on module mass and volume, delivery vehicle capacity, and whether simultaneous deployment is operationally manageable. Engineering analysis will determine the actual delivery scenario.

---

## Priority Rationale

No humans arrive until the base is built. Therefore:

**Glass and titanium come first** — you cannot build domes without them. No dome = no pressurised habitat = no human habitation possible.

**Structural metals come second** — aluminium and basalt fibre enable the supporting structures within and around the domes.

**Life support gases come third** — oxygen, water, and nitrogen must be ready before the first humans arrive, but the domes must exist before they can be filled.

**Everything else follows** — fuel, silicon, advanced manufacturing, and helium-3 are capabilities that expand what the base can do, not prerequisites for habitation.

## Module Sizing Constraint

The maximum size of any module is determined by the delivery vehicle payload fairing and the underground tunnel dimensions. The Falcon Heavy fairing inner diameter is approximately 5.2 metres. Tunnel dimensions must accommodate the largest module that will ever need to be moved underground.

Modules should be designed to fit within a 2 × 2 × 4 metre envelope where possible — small enough to be carried by 4–6 robots cooperatively and to fit through standard tunnel dimensions.

If a module exceeds this size, it must be delivered as components and assembled on the surface or underground by robots.

All modules use the same connection interface standards for:
- **Power:** High-voltage DC bus connection (voltage TBD)
- **Data:** Optical fibre data link, standard protocol
- **Feedstock:** Standardised pipe flanges for bulk material input
