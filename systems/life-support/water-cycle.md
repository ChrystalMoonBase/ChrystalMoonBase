# Water Cycle
## Chrystal Moon Base — Life Support

**Status:** Concept proposal  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Overview

Water is the most versatile and most critical resource in the base. It is consumed by people (drinking, hygiene), used in food production (hydroponics), fills the dome water shielding layer, and is the feedstock for oxygen and hydrogen production. Managing water as a closed loop — recycling nearly all of it — is essential for a permanent base.

---

## Water Sources

**Primary source — lunar ice:** Extracted from nearby cold traps by the M-Water module and stored in underground cisterns. This is the main supply for filling the dome water layers and for initial life support stockpile.

**Secondary source — regolith processing:** Every tonne of regolith processed by any module releases a small amount of water — tens of grams per kilogram, depending on composition. This slow trickle accumulates over years of operation.

**Tertiary source — metabolic water:** Humans produce water metabolically (from food oxidation) and through exhaled breath. This water is captured by the atmosphere management system and returned to the water cycle.

---

## Recycling Loop

**Drinking/hygiene → wastewater treatment → potable water**  
Standard process: filtration, UV sterilisation, reverse osmosis equivalent. ISS demonstrates >93% recovery from wastewater. Target for CMB: >99%.

**Urine → processed water → drinking water**  
The ISS USOS system achieves this. Psychologically challenging for residents initially; becomes normalised quickly. The chemistry is straightforward — it is just water with contaminants.

**Plant transpiration → condensate collection → water supply**  
In farm domes, plants release water vapour. This is collected by the dome atmosphere management system and returned to the water supply. A well-designed hydroponic system can recover >90% of irrigation water this way.

---

## Underground Storage

All water is stored in underground sintered regolith cisterns. Storage underground protects against:
- Evaporative loss (none in sealed cisterns, but underground location provides redundant protection)
- Radiation damage to water chemistry (underground shielding is significant)
- Temperature extremes (underground temperature is stable)

Cistern capacity is sized for several months of base operation without any production — providing resilience against M-Water module failures or processing interruptions.
