# Energy Budget Per Square Metre
## Chrystal Moon Base — Sintering Economics

**Status:** Estimate with high uncertainty  
**Licence:** CC0

---

## Why This Number Matters

The energy required to sinter one square metre of regolith to structural depth determines:
- How long it takes to build a usable floor area
- How much power the system needs
- Whether the Phase 0.2 concept is economically viable in terms of time

---

## Published Estimates

Published laboratory sintering data (primarily Fateri et al. 2019 and Meurisse et al. 2018) suggest energy inputs of approximately **5,000 to 15,000 kWh per cubic metre** of sintered material, depending on the target density, laser wavelength, and regolith composition.

For a sintered layer 10 cm deep (0.1 m³ per m² of surface):
- **Optimistic:** 5,000 kWh/m³ × 0.1 m³ = **500 kWh/m²**
- **Central estimate:** 8,000 kWh/m³ × 0.1 m³ = **800 kWh/m²**
- **Pessimistic:** 15,000 kWh/m³ × 0.1 m³ = **1,500 kWh/m²**

---

## Time to Sinter the First Platform

The first milestone target is a circular platform 8 metres in diameter (area ~50 m²), sintered to a depth of 25 cm.

At 13.5 kW optical output and using the central energy estimate:

**Energy required:** 8,000 kWh/m³ × (50 m² × 0.25 m) = 100,000 kWh  
**Time at 13.5 kW:** 100,000 kWh / 13.5 kW = **7,407 hours ≈ 10 months**

At the optimistic estimate: ~3.7 months  
At the pessimistic estimate: ~18.5 months

A realistic honest range is therefore **4 months to 18 months** for the first platform — the central estimate of 10 months is the planning figure.

---

## Critical Caveat

The energy estimates above are derived from laboratory experiments at small scale. They have not been validated at construction-relevant scales. The actual energy requirement at Peary conditions (vacuum, highland regolith, the specific temperature cycling at 88.6°N) is unknown.

This is the most important uncertainty in the Phase 0.2 timeline. Demonstrator 1 will reduce this uncertainty significantly.

---

## What Happens While Waiting

The sintering floor grows continuously — every hour of operation produces more sintered area. The "first platform" timeline is a milestone, not the only output. Every square centimetre sintered before that milestone is already permanently useful.
