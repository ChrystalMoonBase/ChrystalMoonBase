## Why these are archived, not deleted

These three anchor concepts represent real engineering work. They are preserved here because:

1. The comparison between them is what justified selecting the Rooted Hybrid. Without the comparison, the selection looks arbitrary. With it, it is defensible.

2. Any of these concepts could become relevant again if Phase 0 ground testing reveals unexpected regolith conditions that change the engineering tradeoffs.

3. Open science means showing your work — including the paths not taken.

---

## Summary of tradeoffs

| Concept | Strength | Weakness | Why not selected |
|---|---|---|---|
| **v1 Spike** | Fastest to stability. Simple — no deployment mechanisms. | Depends entirely on regolith density. May stop short of intended depth in loose upper layer. No surface area. | Single point of failure. No redundancy. No clean working zone around base. |
| **v2.0 Pure Bloom** | ~1000 kg lighter than v1. 26.5 m dust-free work zone. Maximum stability factor. | 5-month sintering window. Requires almost perfectly flat 26.5 m landing zone. | Sintering window too long before structure is secure. Landing precision requirement may be unachievable on Peary ridges. |
| **v2.1 Adaptive Bloom** | Same mass savings as v2.0. Better terrain tolerance (up to 7° average slope). | Each articulated rib hinge must deploy reliably after months of cold soak at −150°C in vacuum. 5-month sintering still required. | Mechanism complexity without resolving the core sintering timeline problem. |
| **v2.5 Rooted Hybrid** ✓ | Immediate mechanical lock + permanent sintered bond. Layered redundancy. 6–8 week sintering vs. 5 months. | More complex than v1. | **Selected — strongest practical baseline.** |
