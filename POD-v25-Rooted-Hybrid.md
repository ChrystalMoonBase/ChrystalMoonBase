> *Mechanical anchor for immediate stability. Partial bloom for working area. Targeted sintering for speed. The shortest vulnerability window of all four variants.*

---

## Philosophy

v2.5 combines a light central mechanical anchor with a partial bloom and focused sintering roots. The penetrator provides immediate rough hold. Four to six shell segments provide a medium-large working platform. The robots sinter concentrated plugs and short radial beams at the critical load points — not a full perimeter ring — completing permanent anchorage in 6 to 8 weeks instead of 5 months.

This is currently identified as the strongest practical baseline by the engineering analysis that informed this concept. **That assessment must be validated by qualified engineers with actual geotechnical knowledge of the Peary Crater rim terrain.**

## How It Works

A 120 to 150 kilogram titanium central penetrator drives 0.5 to 0.8 metres into the regolith on a moderate-velocity touchdown — providing immediate rough hold that is not dependent on the bloom sequence being perfect. Four to six hinged shell segments then unfold to form an 18 to 20-metre diameter platform. Compliant passive footpads at the segment ends accommodate local terrain variation.

The robots do not sinter a full perimeter ring. Instead, they concentrate on deep plugs and short radial beams under the central penetrator and at the outer load points — approximately 2 to 3 cubic metres of targeted sintering in the most structurally critical locations.

### Sintering Energy Calculation

```
Rooted sintering: 2.5 m³ × 8,000 kWh/m³ ÷ 13.5 kW optical ≈ 1,481 hours ≈ 8.7 weeks
```

*The 6 to 8 week estimate reflects the smaller total volume and focus on critical zones. The 8,000 kWh/m³ figure requires Phase 0 validation.*

---

## What Makes It Credible

The combination of a mechanical anchor and sintered roots provides genuine redundancy: neither system depends entirely on the other. If the penetrator finds poor ground, the roots compensate. If one root plug fails to reach depth, the penetrator and remaining plugs still hold.

The 6 to 8 week sintering window — versus 5 months for the bloom variants — dramatically reduces the period when the structure is held only by geometry. Finding an 18 to 20-metre acceptable zone at a Peary rim peak is also significantly more likely than finding a 26.5-metre zone, based on the slopes and crater density visible in LRO LOLA data. Fewer hinges than v2.1 keep the deployment sequence simpler.

---

## What Makes It Difficult

The design is more complex than v1 and has more potential failure modes than the pure penetrator approach. Sintering plugs and beams rather than a continuous ring may be more vulnerable to regolith variability — a plug that misses a strong layer achieves less anchoring than intended.

The final working area of 18 to 20 metres is smaller than the bloom variants, though substantially larger than v1.

---

## Primary Risk

**Plug depth variability in heterogeneous regolith.**  
The sintered plug strategy assumes the laser can fuse material to the required depth at the targeted locations. If the regolith at any plug location is unusually loose, dense, or ice-bearing, the plug may not achieve the designed depth. Redundancy from multiple plugs mitigates but does not eliminate this risk.

---

## Best Suited For

The most balanced near-term option. Combines mechanical and sintered anchoring, reduces the vulnerability window to weeks rather than months, and requires a less demanding site. Currently the preferred baseline — pending engineering validation.

---

## Open Questions for Engineers

1. What is the minimum plug depth required for structural equivalence to the full v2.0 ring, under the maximum expected mast loading?
2. What is the optimal plug geometry — cylindrical, conical, or stepped — for maximum pullout resistance in lunar regolith?
3. Does the partial bloom geometry produce adequate stability under the asymmetric loading of a 100-metre mast in a permanent cross-wind from solar pressure?
4. What is the regolith variability risk at the specific candidate Peary rim peaks?
