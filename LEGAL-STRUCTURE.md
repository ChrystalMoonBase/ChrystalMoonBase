## The Short Version

The hardware is open. The software is not. The documents are public domain. The CMB initiative itself is non-profit and makes no profit. The commercial companies that support it — Nexus Ignis B.V. ## 1. The Structure — CMB as an Open-Source Initiative, Borne by Nexus Ignis B.V.

**Chrystal Moon Base is not a separate legal person.** It is an open-source, non-profit initiative — a project and a body of published work — carried by a commercial company:

- **Nexus Ignis B.V.** — a Dutch private limited company (PEEK and high-performance polymer 3D-printing service, Limburg). This is the legal entity behind the initiative. It earns revenue from its commercial activities and bears the costs of developing and publishing CMB openly.
- **A future robotics entity** (working name to be decided) — intended to join as a second commercial pillar, growing out of the CMB8LF robot programme.

**Why a B.V. and not a Stichting (foundation):** A B.V. is legally free to publish, license openly, and give away its own work for nothing — open licensing (CC0, CERN-OHL, GPL) is fully permitted, and the company simply bears the cost. CMB does not need a separate non-profit legal person to be open. Keeping CMB as an initiative under Nexus Ignis B.V. is simpler and avoids setting up a parallel foundation.

**Honest caveat — openness is policy, not an irreversible legal lock.** A Stichting, by its statutes, *cannot* distribute profit; that permanence is its one real advantage. Under a B.V., the commitment to keep CMB open rests on the owner's choice as company policy. A future owner could, in principle, change that policy. We state this plainly rather than imply a guarantee that does not exist. The mitigation is transparency: the open licences already granted (CC0, CERN-OHL-S) are irrevocable for everything already published — once released, it stays released.

**Mission of the CMB initiative:** To research, develop, and openly publish technology for building a permanent human presence on the Moon using in-situ lunar materials, for the benefit of all humanity, with no claim to lunar territory.

**Intellectual property:** CMB intellectual property is currently held by Berechja Kerkdijk and Nexus Ignis B.V. and is published under the open licences below. The hardware and document IP, once published, is open and irrevocably licensed as such.

**No territory claim:** CMB claims no part of the lunar surface. The Outer Space Treaty (1967) Article II prohibits national appropriation of the Moon; CMB applies the same principle to itself and to any private entity.

**ANBI note:** A B.V. cannot hold ANBI (Dutch public-benefit/tax-advantaged) status — that requires a non-profit legal form. CMB does not currently rely on tax-advantaged donations. If ANBI-based donation advantages ever become necessary, a dedicated non-profit vehicle could be established at that point. This is not currently planned.

## 2. What Is Open and What Is Not

### 2a. Hardware Designs — CERN-OHL-S-2.0

The following are open source under the CERN Open Hardware Licence Version 2 — Strongly Reciprocal:

- CMB-R1 robot chassis — mechanical design, leg architecture, tool head interfaces
- The mast structure — segments, deployment mechanism, anchoring variants
- The pod structure — bloom deployment, leaf design, storage chambers
- All dome architecture designs
- All processing module concepts (M-Glass, M-Ti, M-O2, and all others)

**In practice:** You may build a CMB-R1 chassis. You may modify and sell hardware based on these designs. You must credit "Chrystal Moon Base / Nexus Ignis B.V. / Berechja Kerkdijk" and share modifications under the same licence.

Full licence text: [LICENSE-HARDWARE.md](./LICENSE-HARDWARE.md)

### 2b. Documents and Written Content — CC0 1.0 Universal (Public Domain)

All written documents in this repository are released under CC0. No attribution required, though appreciated.

### 2c. Software — Proprietary

The following remain proprietary to Nexus Ignis B.V.:

- All flight software running on robots during lunar operations
- Sintering AI — laser control, scan patterns, quality assessment algorithms
- Swarm coordination software
- Flight-critical guidance and navigation software
- Operational procedures for actual lunar missions
- Test data from physical demonstrator programmes

**No software source code is published in this repository.**

**Why:** Open hardware lets anyone build their own robot. Proprietary software protects the supporting company's ability to sustain the initiative financially over a very long timeframe. This position may change only by published notice with 90 days notice.

Full notice: [LICENSE-SOFTWARE.md](./LICENSE-SOFTWARE.md)

## 3. How Money Works

**CMB itself earns no money.** It claims no territory, sells nothing, and makes no profit. It is a cost centre, not a revenue source.

**Revenue is earned by the commercial companies** — Nexus Ignis B.V. (PEEK/polymer 3D-printing services) and the future robotics entity. These companies operate normally, earn revenue from their products and services, and fund the open CMB initiative as part of their activity and mission.

**Transparency:** As the initiative grows, the relationship between the commercial activity and the open CMB work will be documented openly. There is no internal money system or territory claim anywhere in the CMB concept.

## 5. No Territory Claims
