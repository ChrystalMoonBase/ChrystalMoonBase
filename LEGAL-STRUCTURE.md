# Legal Structure and Transparency
## Chrystal Moon Base — Complete Overview

**Last updated:** 16 June 2026 | Dutch law applies
**Status:** This document describes the intended structure. There is **one legal
entity — Nexus Ignis B.V.** — a Dutch private limited company with **three internal
divisions**: (1) 3D-printing (commercial), (2) robotics (commercial), and (3)
Chrystal Moon Base (CMB), the open-source initiative. The earlier "two-entity"
model (a separate future robotics company) and the Stichting (foundation) plan are
both **dropped**.

---

## The Short Version

There is one company: **Nexus Ignis B.V.**, organised in three divisions —
3D-printing, robotics, and CMB. The 3D-printing and robotics divisions are
commercial and earn the revenue. The **CMB division is open source, with one
exception: the software.** Hardware designs are open (CERN-OHL-S-2.0), documents
are public domain (CC0), and **all control/firmware software is proprietary** and
kept in a separate private repository. CMB itself makes no profit; the commercial
divisions fund it. Nothing here is hidden.

## 1. The Structure — One B.V., Three Divisions

**There is a single legal person: Nexus Ignis B.V.** (a Dutch private limited
company, Limburg), owned by Berechja Kerkdijk. It operates three internal
divisions:

- **3D-printing division (commercial)** — PEEK and high-performance polymer
  3D-printing services. Primary revenue today.
- **Robotics division (commercial)** — grows out of the CMB8LF robot programme;
  intended commercial activity.
- **CMB division (open-source, no revenue)** — researches and openly publishes
  technology for a permanent lunar presence using in-situ materials, for the
  benefit of all humanity, with no claim to lunar territory.

There is **no separate robotics company** and **no Stichting** — both earlier ideas
are dropped. Robotics is a division *inside* Nexus Ignis B.V.

**Why a B.V. and not a Stichting (foundation):** A B.V. is legally free to publish, license openly, and give away its own work for nothing — open licensing (CC0, CERN-OHL) is fully permitted, and the company simply bears the cost. CMB does not need a separate non-profit legal person to be open. Keeping CMB as a division of Nexus Ignis B.V. is simpler and avoids setting up a parallel foundation.

**Honest caveat — openness is policy, not an irreversible legal lock.** A Stichting, by its statutes, *cannot* distribute profit; that permanence is its one real advantage. Under a B.V., the commitment to keep the CMB division open rests on the owner's choice as company policy. A future owner could, in principle, change that policy. We state this plainly rather than imply a guarantee that does not exist. The mitigation is transparency: the open licences already granted (CC0, CERN-OHL-S) are irrevocable for everything already published — once released, it stays released.

**Mission of the CMB division:** To research, develop, and openly publish technology for building a permanent human presence on the Moon using in-situ lunar materials, for the benefit of all humanity, with no claim to lunar territory.

**Intellectual property:** CMB intellectual property is held by Berechja Kerkdijk and Nexus Ignis B.V. The hardware and document IP, once published, is open and irrevocably licensed as such. The software IP is retained (proprietary) and not published.

**No territory claim:** CMB claims no part of the lunar surface. The Outer Space Treaty (1967) Article II prohibits national appropriation of the Moon; CMB applies the same principle to itself and to any private entity.

**ESA note:** For any ESA contract, the **contracting legal party is Nexus Ignis B.V.** (the single entity). CMB is a division of it, not a separate signatory.

**ANBI note:** A B.V. cannot hold ANBI (Dutch public-benefit/tax-advantaged) status — that requires a non-profit legal form. CMB does not currently rely on tax-advantaged donations. If ANBI-based donation advantages ever become necessary, a dedicated non-profit vehicle could be established at that point. This is not currently planned.

## 2. What Is Open and What Is Not

### 2a. Hardware Designs — CERN-OHL-S-2.0

The following are open source under the CERN Open Hardware Licence Version 2 — Strongly Reciprocal:

- CMB8LF robot chassis (CMB8LF-S1 scale and CMB8LF-L1 lunar) — mechanical design, leg architecture, tool head interfaces
- The mast structure — segments, deployment mechanism, anchoring variants
- The pod structure — bloom deployment, leaf design, storage chambers
- All dome architecture designs
- All processing module concepts (M-Glass, M-Aluminium, M-O2, and all others)

**In practice:** You may build a CMB8LF-L1 chassis. You may modify and sell hardware based on these designs. You must credit "Chrystal Moon Base / Nexus Ignis B.V. / Berechja Kerkdijk" and share modifications under the same licence.

Full licence text: [LICENSE-HARDWARE.md](./LICENSE-HARDWARE.md)

### 2b. Documents and Written Content — CC0 1.0 Universal (Public Domain)

All written documents in this repository are released under CC0. No attribution required, though appreciated.

### 2c. Software — Proprietary

The following remain proprietary to Nexus Ignis B.V. and are kept in a SEPARATE PRIVATE repository (not this public repo):

- All flight software running on robots during lunar operations
- Sintering AI — laser control, scan patterns, quality assessment algorithms
- Swarm coordination software
- Flight-critical guidance and navigation software
- Operational procedures for actual lunar missions
- Test data from physical demonstrator programmes

**No software source code is published in this repository.**

**Why:** Open hardware lets anyone build their own robot. Proprietary software protects the supporting company's ability to sustain the initiative financially over a very long timeframe. This position is company policy of the single owner.

Full notice: [LICENSE-SOFTWARE.md](./LICENSE-SOFTWARE.md)

## 3. How Money Works

**CMB itself earns no money.** It claims no territory, sells nothing, and makes no profit. It is a cost centre, not a revenue source.

**Revenue is earned by the commercial companies** — Nexus Ignis B.V. (PEEK/polymer 3D-printing services) and the robotics division. These companies operate normally, earn revenue from their products and services, and fund the open CMB initiative as part of their activity and mission.

**Transparency:** As the initiative grows, the relationship between the commercial activity and the open CMB work will be documented openly. There is no internal money system or territory claim anywhere in the CMB concept.

## 5. No Territory Claims

This initiative claims no ownership of any part of the lunar surface. The Outer Space Treaty (1967) Article II prohibits national appropriation of the Moon. CMB interprets this as applying to private entities as well.

We own hardware designs and publish them openly. We do not own ground.
