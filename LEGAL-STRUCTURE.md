# Legal Structure and Transparency
## Chrystal Moon Base — Complete Overview

**Last updated:** 4 May 2026 | Dutch law applies
**Status:** This document describes intended future structures. The Stichting has not yet been formally incorporated. All structures described are plans, not current legal entities.

---

## The Short Version

The hardware is open. The software is not. The documents are public domain. The mission is non-profit. Future commercial operations will be separate and transparent. Nothing here is hidden, and nothing here is final — this document will be updated as structures are formalised.

---

## 1. The Organisation — Stichting Chrystal Base

**Planned legal form:** Stichting (Dutch foundation), to be incorporated under Dutch law.

A Stichting is a non-profit legal entity with no shareholders and no members. It cannot distribute profits to founders or directors. Its sole purpose is the achievement of its stated mission.

**All intellectual property is currently held by Berechja Kerkdijk and will be transferred to the Stichting upon formal incorporation.**

**Planned mission:** To research, develop, and openly publish technology for building a permanent human presence on the Moon using in-situ lunar materials, for the benefit of all humanity, with no claim to lunar territory.

**What the Stichting intends to own (after incorporation):**
- All open hardware intellectual property
- Physical hardware assets (Pod, mast, robots) if and when built
- Domain names, repository, and brand

**What the Stichting will never claim:**
- Any part of the lunar surface — prohibited by the Outer Space Treaty (1967) Article II and a founding principle of this project

**ANBI status:** The Stichting intends to apply for ANBI (Algemeen Nut Beogende Instelling — Public Benefit Organisation) status with the Dutch tax authority upon incorporation. ANBI status provides enhanced transparency requirements and tax advantages for donors. All financial accounts will be published annually as required.

## 2. What Is Open and What Is Not

### 2a. Hardware Designs — CERN-OHL-S-2.0

The following are intended to be open source under the CERN Open Hardware Licence Version 2 — Strongly Reciprocal:

- CMB-R1 robot chassis — mechanical design, leg architecture, tool head interfaces
- The mast structure — segments, deployment mechanism, anchoring variants
- The pod structure — bloom deployment, leaf design, storage chambers
- All dome architecture designs
- All processing module concepts (M-Glass, M-Ti, M-O2, and all others)

**In practice:** You may build a CMB-R1 chassis. You may modify and sell hardware based on these designs. You must credit "Stichting Chrystal Base / Berechja Kerkdijk" and share modifications under the same licence.

Full licence text: [LICENSE-HARDWARE. md](./LICENSE-HARDWARE. md)

### 2b. Documents and Written Content — CC0 1.0 Universal (Public Domain)

All written documents in this repository — concept papers, technical overviews, one-pagers, governance documents, phase descriptions — are released under CC0. No attribution required, though attribution is appreciated.

### 2c. Software — Proprietary

The following are intended to remain proprietary:

- All flight software running on robots during lunar operations
- Sintering AI — laser control, scan patterns, quality assessment algorithms
- Swarm coordination software
- Flight-critical guidance and navigation software
- Operational procedures for actual lunar missions
- Test data from physical demonstrator programmes

**No software source code is published in this repository.**

**Nothing in the hardware licences (CERN-OHL-S-2.0) or the CC0 document licence grants any rights to the proprietary software.**

**Why:** The hardware being open allows anyone to build their own robot. The software being proprietary protects the organisation's ability to sustain itself financially over what will be a very long timeframe.

This position may change only by public board resolution, published in this repository with 90 days notice.

Full notice: [LICENSE-SOFTWARE. md](./LICENSE-SOFTWARE. md)

## 3. How Money Is Used

All financial accounts of the Stichting will be published annually. Planned allocation of annual operating budget:

| Category | Allocation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hardware R&D and testing | ~70% | Sintering tests, robot prototypes, laser systems |
| Operations | ~20% | Legal, admin, hosting — no marketing budget |
| Founder compensation | ~10% of annual operating budget | Fixed salary, published annually |

**Founder compensation:** Berechja Kerkdijk intends to receive a fixed monthly salary from the Stichting, at a rate to be set and published in the annual financial report. No bonus. No equity. No dividend — a Dutch Stichting cannot legally pay dividends to its founder.

**Donations:** All donations go entirely to the Stichting. No donation provides ownership, voting rights, or any financial return. What donors receive: open hardware designs, open documents, annual financial reports, and the knowledge that the project exists partly because of their support.

## 4. Future Commercial Operations

If the base ever becomes operational, commercial leasing of dome clusters and infrastructure may generate revenue. This commercial activity may be conducted through a separate BV (Dutch private company) established at the appropriate time.

**The intended separation:**
- **Stichting** — mission, research, open hardware, non-profit
- **BV** (future, if needed) — commercial infrastructure leasing

**BV governance guardrail:** Any future BV using Stichting IP must have at least one independent board member approved by the Stichting's Raad van Toezicht.

**Revenue flow:** Commercial revenue from any future BV would flow back to the Stichting to fund further base expansion. This structure and all financial flows will be published openly when established.

**Founder compensation from BV:** The founder may receive compensation from a future BV. Any such compensation shall be market-rate, approved by the Stichting's Raad van Toezicht, and published annually. No majority shareholding by the founder if the BV uses Stichting IP.

## 5. No Territory Claims

This project claims no ownership of any part of the lunar surface. The Outer Space Treaty (1967) Article II prohibits national appropriation of the Moon. CMB interprets this as applying to private entities as well.

We own hardware. We do not own ground.
