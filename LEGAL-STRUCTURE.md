# Legal Structure and Transparency
## Chrystal Moon Base — Complete Overview

**Last updated:** 4 May 2026 | Dutch law applies 
**Status:** This document describes intended future structures. The Stichting has not yet been formally incorporated. All structures described are plans, not current legal entities.

---

## The Short Version

The hardware is open. The software is not. The mission is non-profit. Future commercial operations will be separate and transparent. Nothing here is hidden, and nothing here is final — this document will be updated as structures are formalised.

---

## 1. The Organisation — Stichting Chrystal Moon Base

**Planned legal form:** Stichting (Dutch foundation), to be incorporated under Dutch law.

A Stichting is a non-profit legal entity with no shareholders and no members. It cannot distribute profits to founders or directors. Its sole purpose is the achievement of its stated mission.

**Planned mission:** To research, develop, and openly publish technology for building a permanent human presence on the Moon using in-situ lunar materials, for the benefit of all humanity, with no claim to lunar territory.

**What the Stichting intends to own:**
- All open hardware intellectual property
- Physical hardware assets (Pod, mast, robots) if and when built
- Domain names, repository, and brand

**What the Stichting will never claim:**
- Any part of the lunar surface — prohibited by the Outer Space Treaty (1967) Article II and a founding principle of this project

## 2. What Is Open and What Is Not

### Open — CERN-OHL-S-2.0

The following are intended to be open source under the CERN Open Hardware Licence Version 2 — Strongly Reciprocal:

- CMB-R1 robot chassis — mechanical design, leg architecture, tool head interfaces
- The mast structure — segments, deployment mechanism, anchoring variants
- The pod structure — bloom deployment, leaf design, storage chambers
- All dome architecture designs
- All processing module concepts (M-Glass, M-Ti, M-O2, and all others)
- All documents in this repository

**In practice:** You may build a CMB-R1 chassis. You may modify and sell hardware based on these designs. You must credit "Stichting Chrystal Moon Base / Berechja Kerkdijk" and share modifications under the same licence.

Full licence text: https://ohwr. org/cernohls_v2. txt

### Not Open — Proprietary

The following are intended to remain proprietary:

- All flight software running on robots during lunar operations
- Sintering AI — laser control, scan patterns, quality assessment algorithms
- Swarm coordination software
- Flight-critical guidance and navigation software
- Operational procedures for actual lunar missions
- Test data from physical demonstrator programmes

**Why:** The hardware being open allows anyone to build their own robot. The software being proprietary protects the organisation's ability to sustain itself financially over what will be a very long timeframe. The hardware is the idea. The software is the execution. Both are necessary — only one needs to be protected.

This position may change only by public board resolution, published in this repository with 90 days notice. If the Stichting becomes self-sustaining through other means, the software licence will be reviewed. Any change will be announced publicly in this repository.

## 3. How Money Is Used

All financial accounts of the Stichting will be published annually. Planned allocation:

| Category | Allocation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hardware R&D and testing | ~70% | Sintering tests, robot prototypes, laser systems |
| Operations | ~20% | Legal, admin, hosting — no marketing budget |
| Founder compensation | ~10% | Fixed salary, published annually |

**Founder compensation:** Berechja Kerkdijk intends to receive a fixed monthly salary from the Stichting, at a rate to be set and published in the annual financial report. No bonus. No equity. No dividend — a Dutch Stichting cannot legally pay dividends to its founder.

**Donations:** All donations go entirely to the Stichting. No donation provides ownership, voting rights, or any financial return. What donors receive: open blueprints, annual financial reports, and the knowledge that the project exists partly because of their support.

## 4. Future Commercial Operations

If the base ever becomes operational, commercial leasing of dome clusters and infrastructure may generate revenue. This commercial activity may be conducted through a separate BV (Dutch private company) established at the appropriate time.

**The intended separation:**
- **Stichting** — mission, research, open hardware, non-profit
- **BV** (future, if needed) — commercial infrastructure leasing

**Revenue flow:** Commercial revenue from any future BV would flow back to the Stichting to fund further base expansion — Pod #2, Pod #3, and beyond. This structure and all financial flows will be published openly when established.

The founder may receive compensation from a future BV. Any founder compensation from a future BV shall be market-rate, approved by the Stichting's Raad van Toezicht, and published annually. No majority shareholding by founder if BV uses Stichting IP.

---

## 5. No Territory Claims

This project claims no ownership of any part of the lunar surface. The Outer Space Treaty (1967) Article II prohibits national appropriation of the Moon. CMB interprets this as applying to private entities as well.

We own hardware. We do not own ground.
